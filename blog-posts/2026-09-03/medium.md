# B2B SaaS Marketing Tactics Happen Before Your Funnel Starts

*Why 67% of B2B decisions happen before your marketing even starts*

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KaranSud/marketing-portfolio/main/blog-posts/2026-09-03/hero.jpg)

Most B2B SaaS marketing teams are optimizing a funnel that 67% of their buyers will never enter.

That is not a conversion rate problem. It is not an ICP problem, a messaging problem, or a failure to A/B test the right headline. It is a structural problem with how the whole game was designed, and the companies that have figured this out are doing something that looks almost counterintuitive from the outside: they are spending less on demand capture and more on what happens before any demand is visible.

This post is about what that actually looks like in practice, and why the distinction matters more in 2026 than it did even two years ago.

## The Problem Is Not Your Conversion Rate

Here is the standard B2B SaaS marketing playbook, still running at most companies: you drive traffic through paid search and content, you capture leads via gated assets and demo requests, you hand MQLs to sales, and you optimize each step until the numbers improve.

The logic is internally consistent. The problem is the premise.

That playbook was built for a buying process where vendors could intercept buyers at the moment of active search. That moment existed when search was slow, when category education happened through vendor-controlled channels (white papers, webinars, sales calls), and when buyers needed a sales rep to get product information they could not find elsewhere.

None of those conditions hold anymore.

When a VP of Revenue Operations is evaluating project management tools for her team, she is not starting with a Google search that leads to your landing page. She is asking her network in a Pavilion Slack channel what they use. She is running a query through Claude or ChatGPT and getting a landscape summary. She is noticing which vendors get mentioned repeatedly by people she respects on LinkedIn. By the time she visits your website, she has a buyer shortlist. The question is whether you are on it.

## Where 67% of B2B Buying Actually Happens

In March 2026, Gartner published the results of a survey of 646 B2B buyers who had completed a purchase in the prior twelve months. The finding that landed hardest: [67% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free buying experience](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) for at least part of their purchase process.

Read that with the emphasis it deserves. Not that buyers dislike reps. Not that digital is preferred for small deals. The majority of B2B buyers (across deal sizes and industries) want a meaningful portion of their journey to happen without talking to a sales representative.

The McKinsey B2B Pulse Survey published in 2024 fills in why: [B2B buyers now use an average of 10 interaction channels](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/five-fundamental-truths-how-b2b-winners-keep-growing) across all stages of their buying journey, up from five channels in 2016. Those channels are not in your marketing dashboard. They are peer conversations, AI research sessions, community threads, private messages, review sites, and podcasts, each one leaving no trackable signal when it influences a decision.

> 📊 Chart: B2B buyer channel usage in 2016 vs 2024, showing growth from 5 to 10 average channels per buyer journey — [View interactive chart](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KaranSud/marketing-portfolio/main/blog-posts/2026-09-03/chart1.html)

This is not the dark funnel as a mysterious, unmeasurable thing. This is the dark funnel as the actual decision arena. The traditional tracked funnel (paid ads, form fills, MQL handoffs) is increasingly a late-stage confirmation of a preference that formed somewhere else.

## What "Rep-Free" Means for Your Marketing Stack

The shift gets more structural when you look at the dollar thresholds involved.

McKinsey's data shows that [39% of B2B buyers are now willing to complete purchases of $500,000 or more](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/five-fundamental-truths-how-b2b-winners-keep-growing) through self-service digital commerce or remote connections, up from 28% just two years prior. That is an 11-point jump in two years for enterprise-level deals.

Forrester saw this coming. In October 2024, the firm predicted that by 2025, [more than half of B2B transactions of $1 million or greater would be processed through digital self-serve channels](https://investor.forrester.com/news-releases/news-release-details/forresters-b2b-marketing-sales-predictions-2025-more-half-large), through the vendor's website or a marketplace.

These are not SMB subscriptions. These are seven-figure deals closing without a rep owning the relationship through most of the process. That fundamentally changes what marketing is responsible for building.

If the buying process for your largest deals is happening through self-serve paths and digital channels, then the value of marketing is not primarily in generating leads that sales converts. It is in building the brand presence, authority, and reputation that causes a buyer to put you on their shortlist before the self-serve journey even begins.

> 📊 Chart: Percentage of B2B buyers willing to complete self-serve purchases at each deal size tier, comparing 2022 to 2024 with the largest jump at the 00K+ level — [View interactive chart](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KaranSud/marketing-portfolio/main/blog-posts/2026-09-03/chart2.html)

The tactical implication is uncomfortable if you built your team around MQL volume: most of the work that determines whether you win enterprise deals is happening in channels you cannot attribute, track, or optimize in Salesforce.

## The Five Channels Where B2B Shortlists Are Built

> 🗺 Infographic: The five dark funnel channels where B2B shortlists are built before buyers enter any vendor's tracked funnel, with what to do in each — [View infographic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KaranSud/marketing-portfolio/main/blog-posts/2026-09-03/infographic.svg)

The channels where pre-decision influence actually accumulates are not secret. They are just different from what most B2B SaaS marketing teams are resourced to operate.

Peer communities are the most direct. Buyers trust the opinions of practitioners who have actually used the software. Private Slack groups, LinkedIn communities, Pavilion chapters, Reddit threads in category-adjacent subreddits. These are where recommendations form. Being a named, credible voice in two or three relevant communities creates recall that paid ads cannot replicate.

AI research is the channel that changed most dramatically in the last eighteen months. The Gartner survey found that 45% of buyers used AI during their recent purchase, and separate research from 2026 puts the share of B2B buyers who begin vendor research inside an AI chatbot at over half. The implication is specific: AI models need to be able to say accurate, positive things about your product when asked. That requires third-party citations (review sites, published research, media coverage, authoritative content) because models pull from what exists in the indexed web and what other credible sources say about you.

Peer review platforms (G2, TrustRadius, Capterra, Gartner Peer Insights) function as standing testimonials. Buyers read them before they read your website, and AI systems cite them when answering research queries. A systematic process for surfacing satisfied customers to review platforms is not a nice-to-have; it is infrastructure.

Executive and founder content builds category authority before any individual deal. Podcasts, LinkedIn posts, YouTube videos, newsletters. The mechanism is simple: consistent publication of opinionated, specific perspectives on your category. Gong built a large share of its brand authority through original research reports and a podcast before most of its eventual customers ever interacted with a sales rep. The Similarweb data tells the story bluntly: direct and dark traffic accounts for [72.1% of visits to Gong's website](https://www.similarweb.com/blog/marketing/marketing-strategy/dark-funnel-b2b/) and 71.6% of visits to HubSpot's. That traffic came from somewhere, and it was not Google Ads.

Dark social is every private share and message that your analytics reads as direct traffic. Content shared in Slack, LinkedIn DMs, WhatsApp groups, and internal email threads drives significant brand exposure with zero attribution signal. The only way to influence it is to create content that people want to share with their specific colleagues, not content optimized to rank.

## Why 45% of Buyers Now Use AI Before They Use Your Website

The Gartner finding that [45% of B2B buyers used AI during their purchase](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) lands differently when you understand what that usage looks like in practice.

A buyer does not ask an AI for a demo. They ask things like: "What are the main project management tools for enterprise software teams, and what do practitioners think of each?" The AI answers from its training data, citing what the indexed web says about each vendor. If your company is mentioned primarily in your own marketing copy, the model has little to work with. If your company appears repeatedly in G2 reviews, third-party articles, analyst reports, conference talks, and community discussions, the model has a well-formed picture to draw from.

This is what "brand" means in 2026 for B2B SaaS: not recognition from advertising, but distributed presence across the sources of truth that AI systems and buyers use to form opinions. The companies treating this as an SEO tactic (optimizing for AEO by stuffing their own content with keywords) are getting it backwards. The signal that matters is third-party attestation, not self-assertion.

The buyer journey now frequently looks like this: colleague mentions a vendor in a Slack conversation, buyer asks an AI about that vendor, AI confirms strong reputation with specific evidence, buyer searches the brand name directly, and arrives at your website already 70% decided. The attribution model reads the last step and credits branded search. Marketing gets measured on a conversion that was determined much earlier.

## How to Build Presence in Channels You Cannot Track

This is where the B2B SaaS marketing tactics that actually move the shortlist needle diverge from the playbook that fills dashboards.

The practical program looks less like a campaign and more like a publication strategy. You are building a body of work (original research, strong POV content, community participation, review infrastructure, executive visibility) that compounds over time. Each piece of it does something specific in the dark funnel.

Original research or proprietary data is the highest-return asset because it gets cited. When you publish a state-of-the-market report or a benchmark study drawn from your actual customer base, other publications reference it, AI models cite it, community members share it. That creates the distributed presence that forms reputation before a buyer knows they need your category.

Community participation means having named individuals at your company (not a branded account, but actual people with opinions) who contribute regularly to spaces where your buyers congregate. This takes time and cannot be automated. It also cannot be replicated by a competitor who starts next quarter. Six months of consistent community presence creates recall that no campaign budget can buy.

Review site infrastructure is the most underinvested area in most B2B SaaS marketing budgets. Building a systematic process for identifying satisfied customers and making it easy for them to leave reviews on G2, TrustRadius, or Gartner Peer Insights costs very little relative to the influence those reviews have on buyers doing pre-decision research.

Podcast and video content builds recall at scale. A buyer who has listened to your founder or your VP of product for ten hours has a formed opinion before they enter your funnel. The content does not need to be promotional. It needs to be genuinely useful to the practitioner audience you are trying to reach.

## The Long Game and Why It Will Pay Off

There is a counterintuitive finding in the Gartner research worth sitting with. In August 2025, Gartner published a prediction: by 2030, [75% of B2B buyers will prefer sales experiences that prioritize human interaction over AI](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-25-gartner-says-by-2030-that-75-percent-of-b2b-buyers-will-prefer-sales-experiences-that-prioritize-human-interaction-over-ai).

The pattern this points to is useful: digital and AI-mediated channels are winning the early stages of the buying journey, while human connection is becoming more important, not less, at the closing stages. This means that B2B SaaS marketing tactics need to work in two registers simultaneously: a long-form, slow-burn program that builds pre-funnel presence across peer and AI channels, and a high-quality human-driven experience at the point when buyers do choose to engage with your team.

The companies that lose in this environment are the ones who respond to declining funnel metrics by pouring more budget into demand capture: more paid search, more outbound sequences, more SDR capacity. They are fighting for the 33% of buyers still in an accessible search phase while their category is being decided in Slack channels and AI conversations they cannot see.

The companies that win are treating dark funnel marketing as a primary strategic investment, not a brand awareness program that gets cut when revenue targets slip. They are publishing consistently, participating in communities, building review infrastructure, and measuring success through indicators that do not show up in a CRM: branded search growth, direct traffic volume, community mentions, and the quality of conversations that sales reps are having with buyers who arrive already knowing exactly what they want.

## What This Means for Your Marketing Plan on Monday

Stop treating dark funnel presence as a PR activity and start resourcing it as infrastructure. Any credible B2B pipeline strategy in 2026 needs a pre-funnel component, not just a demand-capture stack. Assign someone on your team to own community participation and give them six months before measuring pipeline outcomes. Commission original research from your customer base or proprietary data. Audit your review site presence across G2, TrustRadius, and Gartner Peer Insights and build a systematic process to surface satisfied customers.

At the same time, be honest about what your current MQL metrics are measuring. They are measuring the last touchpoint before a decision that was probably made weeks earlier. That is useful data, but it is not a proxy for where your pipeline is actually being built.

Build presence where shortlists are made, which means showing up in conversations your attribution software will never credit and trusting that it works before you can prove it.
