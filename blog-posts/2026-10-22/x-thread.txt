1/ A quarter of your email list has already gone dark.

Not bounced. Not unsubscribed. Just silent — sitting there counted in your list size and routed into every automation you've built.

Here's what that actually costs you (and most teams have no idea):

2/ Email list decay runs at 28% annually.

That's the verified rate from ZeroBounce's 2025 report, which analyzed real email verification data from 2024. The trend: 23% in 2021 → 22% → 25% → 28% peak in 2024.

A 100k list is losing ~77 valid contacts per day. Every day.

3/ The math nobody runs:

100,000 contacts × 28% decay = 28,000 gone per year
= 2,300 per month
= 77 per day

Unless acquisition is adding that many verified subscribers, your real list is shrinking while your reported list size grows.

4/ Here's where it gets worse.

Those inactive addresses don't just fail to convert. Some of them — 0.05% to 0.15% — hit the spam button instead of unsubscribing.

Google's complaint threshold is 0.1%. Above 0.3% sustained: filtering and blocking.

Your decayed list is filing a complaint against your domain.

5/ The feedback loop that eats programs:

Decay → spam signals → lower inbox placement → less engagement → weaker sender reputation → harder to re-engage the contacts still left → decay accelerates.

This is not a one-time cleanup problem. It compounds.

6/ And yet 39% of email senders rarely or never clean their lists.

That's from Sinch Mailgun's State of Email Deliverability survey of 1,100+ senders in 2025.

Only 24% use sunset policies — the automated rule that suppresses contacts who haven't engaged in 90 days.

7/ The monitoring gap is even worse:

→ 70% don't use Google Postmaster Tools
→ 53% don't monitor blocklists

Postmaster Tools is free. It shows your domain's spam rate exactly as Gmail sees it. Not using it is choosing to fly blind.

8/ Why does this keep happening?

List hygiene doesn't show up in open rate reports. Sunset policies don't generate conversions you can point to in a quarterly review.

The incentive structure rewards more sends. The maintenance work keeps getting deprioritized.

9/ The consequence, documented:

Spam placement rates across commercial email nearly doubled between Q1 and Q4 2024 — from 4.5% to 8.6%.

That spike tracked exactly with AI-driven volume increases. Programs ramped sending without touching list quality. The inbox providers noticed.

10/ The fix is four ongoing operations, not one annual campaign:

1. Verify at point of capture — API check on every new signup
2. Sunset policy — 90 days inactive → re-engage → 30 days no response → suppress
3. Reputation monitoring — Postmaster Tools + MS JMRP, weekly
4. Quarterly review — bounce rate, complaint rate, engagement distribution

11/ On the ROI math everyone cites:

The industry says email returns $36–$42 per dollar spent. That figure is real — in programs where it's measured.

A program with 28% decay and no hygiene workflow isn't getting $42 on the decayed portion. It's getting negative returns, because those contacts are actively degrading placement for the other 72%.

12/ The underlying formula: reach × engagement × conversion.

Decay hits reach directly.
Spam signals hit engagement indirectly through inbox placement.
The compounding works against you until you fix the list.

Email's compound returns work exactly like interest, but only if the principal — your list — is real.
