1/ Three in four marketing leaders say their measurement systems are failing.

They spent this year making them worse.

Here's the measurement paradox nobody is talking about 🧵

2/ The IAB surveyed 400+ senior analytics decision-makers.

75% say their attribution, incrementality tests, and MMM models underperform.

These aren't teams that don't measure. They measure carefully and are still wrong.

3/ What changed? The tools got cheap.

Google released Meridian (open-source MMM). Meta released Robyn. PyMC-Marketing built a full Bayesian MMM in Python.

The price went from expensive to free almost overnight.

4/ 46.9% of U.S. brand and agency marketers now plan to invest in MMM next year. (TransUnion/eMarketer 2026)

Adoption is surging. So is overconfidence.

5/ Julian Runge (Northwestern, co-author of Robyn research) put it plainly:

The barrier didn't disappear. It moved.

From "can we access this?" to "do we know what we're doing with it?"

6/ MSI research on open-source MMM tools found the same thing:

Automated models may conceal model misspecifications.

A model that runs without errors doesn't mean the model is correct. It means the model ran.

7/ The 3 most common MMM errors:

① Attributing revenue to paid spend during high-organic-demand periods
② Training on 90-day windows (systematically undervalues brand)
③ Treating correlation as causation

All three produce confident wrong answers.

8/ Error #2 creates a feedback loop:

Short window → undervalue brand → cut brand budget → long-term performance drops → "brand doesn't work" confirmed

The model generated the conclusion it was set up to find.

9/ The measurement architecture that works uses 3 layers:

• Attribution → which campaigns convert today
• Incrementality testing → did we cause this, or would it have happened anyway
• MMM → long-term revenue contribution by channel

Each answers a different question.

10/ None of the three substitutes for the other two.

Attribution without incrementality: confidently credits channels for sales they didn't cause.

MMM without incrementality: correlation presented as strategy.

Incrementality alone: no macro picture.

11/ IAB State of Data 2026: only 39% of marketing teams run all three layers.

The other 61% are drawing conclusions their stacks cannot support.

McKinsey: effective analytics frees up 15-20% of marketing spend. That's not available to teams using one layer.

12/ Sequence matters. You can't build all three at once.

① Attribution first (UTMs + pixel + CRM)
② Incrementality second (geo holdout, 4-6 weeks)
③ MMM third (18+ months of data, incrementality as calibration)

13/ Gartner (Feb 2026): by 2027, over 40% of CMOs pushing for larger brand budgets will lose C-suite influence.

84% of companies are already stuck in a measurement doom loop.

This is a CMO credibility problem now.

14/ McKinsey State of Marketing Europe 2026:

Only 3% of CMOs can explain more than 50% of their spend through MROI measurement.

42% plan to invest more in MROI tools.

Buying more tools is not the fix.

15/ The measurement architecture you need already exists.

What it requires isn't a tool purchase. It's a reckoning with which questions your current models cannot answer.
