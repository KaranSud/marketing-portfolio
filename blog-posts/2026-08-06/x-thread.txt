1/ A peer-reviewed study proves ChatGPT is making brand marketing content measurably more similar. And consumer engagement rises when that access is removed. This is the data no one is talking about.

2/ Italy accidentally ran the experiment for us. When regulators banned ChatGPT in April 2023, Milan restaurants' content became 15% less lexically similar — and engagement went up 3.5%. Less AI. More distinctiveness. Better results.

3/ That study (Liu, Wang & Yang, SSRN July 2025) is the first causal proof of AI content homogenization in marketing. Not an anecdote. A natural experiment. The direction is clear: AI compresses brand voice toward the mean.

4/ Here's why this happens architecturally. Every LLM is trained on existing text. It becomes very good at producing the statistical center of that distribution. Not your brand's voice. The average of most brand voices. Your competitors get the same output.

5/ Meanwhile, 87% of marketers now use AI to create content (Ahrefs, 879-marketer survey). When everyone pulls from the same statistical gravity, the content market gets flatter. Faster, yes. More distinctive, no.

6/ The productivity math looks bad when you run it carefully. AI helped companies publish 42% more content monthly, but growth was only 5% faster. You're adding enormous volume for a marginal lift. Volume is not the lever.

7/ Now add the zero-click problem. 68.01% of US Google searches in early 2026 ended without a click (SparkToro/Similarweb). That's up from 60.45% in 2024. Content that "ranks" increasingly serves readers who never visit your site.

8/ What gets cited inside AI Overviews? Primary sources. Original research. Content cited by trade publications and analysts. Not your 300th AI-assisted blog post. The brands winning in AI search are primary sources, not volume publishers.

9/ The CMI B2B 2025 study of 1,015 marketers found that teams publishing original proprietary research report 64% higher conversion rates than those publishing generic content. That gap is not marginal. It reorganizes budget decisions.

10/ Original research is the one content asset no language model can replicate. An LLM can write about SaaS churn. It cannot tell your buyers you surveyed 400 CFOs and found implementation complexity ranks ahead of price as the churn driver. That's yours.

11/ Starting an original research program is more accessible than most teams think. You need a hypothesis your buyers actually argue about, a defensible sample of 200-400 qualified respondents, and one finding counter-intuitive enough to repeat. That's it.

12/ The productive AI content workflow for 2026: use AI to repurpose and distribute findings that come from your own research. AI as distribution layer. Your proprietary data as the irreplicable asset. Not the other way around.

13/ Gartner forecasts 60% of brands will use agentic AI for one-to-one interactions by 2028. The AI infrastructure keeps building. The brands that invest in original data now will be the ones the AI cites later.

14/ The budget question is simple: what if you published 20% less AI-assisted content and ran one original research study per quarter instead? The CMI data strongly suggests the answer favors the research.
