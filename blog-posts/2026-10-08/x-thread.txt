1/ Your SaaS pipeline metrics look healthy. MQLs up. Demos booked. Pipeline coverage solid.

And your CAC payback keeps getting worse anyway.

This is not a pipeline problem. Here's what's actually happening:

2/ The median B2B SaaS company now spends $2.00 to acquire every $1.00 of new ARR.

That number is up 14% year over year.

For comparison: expansion ARR costs $1.00 per $1.00.

Most growth plans are built backwards.

3/ The CAC payback trend line is brutal.

Median payback stretched from 13 months in 2021 to 18.5 months by 2025.

That's a 42% deterioration in under four years.

While B2B sales cycles got 29% longer in the same window.

4/ The companies that improved payback during this period didn't do it by generating more pipeline.

They did it by generating more precise pipeline.

Fewer accounts. Higher confidence on each one. Structurally short cycles.

5/ Here's the mechanism nobody names directly.

When a buying committee grows, every deal costs more to close. You're running the same conversations across more people and more organizational functions.

This is the primary driver of sales cycle expansion over the past four years.

6/ Gartner surveyed 632 B2B buyers in May 2025.

74% of buying teams show "unhealthy conflict" during the decision process.

Buying groups range from 5 to 16 people across up to four organizational functions.

5 stakeholders and 16 stakeholders are not the same deal.

7/ If your ICP systematically attracts accounts in the upper half of that 5-to-16 range, your fully loaded cost per closed deal is structurally higher.

Even if your win rate is flat.

Adding more pipeline just fills the funnel with more expensive deals.

8/ This is not a channel efficiency problem.

Optimizing your Google campaigns doesn't reduce how many humans need to approve your contract.

It doesn't shorten the procurement review.

It doesn't eliminate the security questionnaire that adds three weeks to every enterprise deal.

9/ Tomasz Tunguz put it cleanly: "If your sales cycle doubles, your bookings are cut in half."

The inverse is less obvious: adding more deals to a long-cycle funnel doesn't fix the bookings problem.

It amplifies the cost structure that created it.

10/ The segmentation data makes the ICP choice visible.

Enterprise (ACV above $100K): 24-month CAC payback.
Mid-market ($15K–$100K): 16 months.
SMB (under $5K): 9 months.

A 2.7x gap. Driven by deal structure, not channel strategy.

11/ McKinsey analyzed 100+ B2B SaaS companies in November 2025.

Top-quartile companies: 24x enterprise value to revenue multiple.
Bottom-quartile: 5x.

Net revenue retention drives that gap. And NRR is the downstream consequence of selling to the right customers in the first place.

12/ The practical fix is a spreadsheet, not a strategy retreat.

Pull 24 months of closed-won deals. Segment by customer type and buying structure. Calculate CAC and payback per cohort separately.

Most teams find one or two cohorts driving all the efficiency — and a pipeline strategy optimized for the others.

13/ Three moves for the next 90 days:

1. Segment your CAC by cohort, not blended (1 week)
2. Add decision-structure criteria to your qualification scoring (2 weeks)
3. Shift a meaningful portion of pipeline budget toward expansion ARR (next quarter)

14/ The obstacle is cultural, not analytical.

Growth teams are measured on pipeline volume, not pipeline efficiency.

Changing the measurement is harder than running another campaign. That's why most teams never do it.

15/ The companies that figure this out in the next two quarters will spend the next two years compounding their advantage over everyone still convinced that more pipeline is the answer.
