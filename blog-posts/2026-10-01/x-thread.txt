1/ The man who built marketing automation's dominant platform just declared the entire architecture obsolete.

Jon Miller, Marketo co-founder, came out of stealth to say rules-based automation is the wrong model. Here's why the data backs him up. 🧵

2/ Forrester predicted B2B companies would lose $10B+ in 2026 from ungoverned AI use.

Not from failing to adopt AI.
From deploying it without governance structures.

AI doesn't create problems. It amplifies whatever's already broken—faster.

3/ 19% of buyers using AI-assisted purchasing tools reported feeling LESS confident in their decisions.

Citing inaccurate AI-generated information.

1 in 5 of your prospects is getting less confident in you because of how your AI performs.

4/ Most marketing automation platforms in 2026 still run on the same architecture as 2009.

You write rules → define conditions → set branches → launch workflows.

The system executes exactly what you told it. That's the problem.

5/ Rules have real strengths: auditable, deterministic, certifiable.

But they don't generalize. They handle every situation you anticipated. They handle poorly every situation you didn't.

The catalog of rules in a typical enterprise stack is a geological record of urgent past ideas.

6/ Miller's argument with Phave:

Rules = wrong instrument for most of what marketing automation should do.
Reasoning models have crossed the threshold where you can do better.

"Consent and quiet hours should be rules. Reasoning models can make the choice now."

7/ McKinsey's agentic AI research found organizations that rebuilt workflows—not just added AI on top—are seeing 10-15x faster campaign creation.

Not 10-15% improvement.
10-15 TIMES.

8/ The adoption gap is brutal.

~90% of CMOs are testing AI applications.

Fewer than 10% have deployed end-to-end agentic workflows generating verifiable business impact.

Only 6% are extracting meaningful bottom-line value.

9/ Why the gap? McKinsey found AI high performers are nearly 3x more likely to have redesigned workflows end-to-end.

55% of high performers vs 20% of everyone else.

Not added AI to existing workflows. Rebuilt the workflows around what AI can own.

10/ Gartner survey of 402 CMOs: AI automation of marketing work expected to go from 16% today to 36% by 2028.

That's a doubling in two years.

Question is whether your architecture will support that scale—or you'll plateau at 22%.

11/ The two-layer stack:

LAYER 1 (Rules): Opt-out, consent, frequency caps, brand standards, legal requirements. Auditable. Stays as rules.

LAYER 2 (Agent): Content selection, timing, channel mix, sequence order, scoring. Computed per-person, per-moment.

12/ Three principles of the next stack:

→ Rules govern, agents decide
→ Workflow redesign comes before tool purchase (3-6 month project)
→ AI governance is a marketing leadership responsibility—not IT's problem

13/ Quick audit for your stack:

Pull active workflows. Classify each as compliance rule or optimization decision.

Most teams find 30-40% are real compliance rules.
The rest? Optimization decisions written as rules because rules were the only tool available.

14/ The structural shift from rules-based to agentic marketing is happening regardless.

Jon Miller coming out of stealth to say rules-based automation is architecturally wrong isn't a lone voice.

It's the creator of the incumbent category announcing the successor is ready.

15/ The question isn't whether your current stack will be replaced.

It's whether you'll lead that replacement or be surprised by it.
