# Article headers that earn the click (12 Nov 2026)

Karan: the old headers (paper, streamlines, full title) "do not have any significance, neither will they get attention or CTR".

## What the evidence says
No controlled study isolates every variable, so this leans on the points most sources agree on, plus how the image is actually seen.

1. **Complement the title, never repeat it.** The title already shows under the image on LinkedIn, X, Slack and on this site. Text that repeats the title wastes the space (vidIQ, screenhance, SEOJuice; YouTube thumbnail guides say the same).
2. **2 to 5 big words, or a single number.** Previews show at about 300px wide on phones, so text has to read at that size. Guides converge on a 5 to 10 word cap for OG images and 2 to 5 words for thumbnails.
3. **High contrast against the feed.** LinkedIn and X feeds are white or near black. A paper-cream card blends into a white feed; a dark card with one bright accent stands out in light mode and keeps its edge in dark mode.
4. **A face builds trust for a personal brand.** Thumbnail guides credit faces with higher CTR (the figures cited, 20 to 38%, have no primary source, so treat them as direction, not fact). On a personal site the face also shows whose analysis it is.
5. **One concrete, surprising number.** Data-led headlines ("58% of opens are machines") create a curiosity gap that the article closes. Newsletters that grow on LinkedIn (Lenny's, Kyle Poyar's Growth Unhinged, Elena Verna) lead with the stat or the chart, not decoration.
6. **Draw the number, don't decorate it.** A 100-dot grid or two bars makes the stat readable before the words are read, and it is the subject's own data, not a generic graphic.

## The pattern we use
- Dark ink card, 1200x630.
- Left: topic in sentence case, the stat in huge Fraunces, one complementary line (max about 8 words, no full stop).
- Right: the stat drawn (dot grid for a percentage, two bars for a before/after or A vs B, people dots for a count).
- Bottom: Karan's face, name, "Engineer turned marketer".
- Posts with no number (the build posts) get a short claim set large instead.
- Hooks live in `portfolio-v3/lib/hooks.ts`, one per slug, each taken from the post's own numbers.
